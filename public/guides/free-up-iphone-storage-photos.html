How to free up iPhone storage from photos

Check what your photos and videos are using, then work through the options from least effort to most.

Updated November 18, 2026

Short answer: Go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage to see how much space Photos uses. If you use iCloud Photos, turn on Optimize iPhone Storage so your iPhone keeps smaller versions and iCloud keeps the originals. Then merge duplicates and delete the photos and videos you don't need.

Check how much space Photos is using

Open Settings > General > iPhone Storage. Apple's storage page covers this view: a bar across the top shows what's filling the phone, and the list below shows how much each app uses. Find Photos in that list. If it's a big share of your storage, the steps below will make a real difference.

Write the number down. You can put it into the photo storage calculator to see how much a cleanup would free.

Turn on Optimize iPhone Storage

If you use iCloud Photos, this is the biggest single win and it doesn't delete anything. With it on, your iPhone keeps space-saving versions of your photos and videos, while the full-resolution originals stay in iCloud.

  • iOS 27 or later: Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos, tap iPhone Storage, and make sure Optimize iPhone Storage is on.
  • iOS 26 or earlier: Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos, then choose Optimize Storage directly on that screen.

Optimize only helps the phone. Your originals still fill iCloud, and iCloud gives you 5 GB for free. If iCloud is full too, the next steps help with both.

Merge duplicate photos

The Photos app finds exact duplicates for you. Open Photos, tap Collections, then Utilities, then Duplicates. Tap Merge on a set, then confirm.

If there's no Duplicates album, you may not have any, or the phone hasn't finished looking. Detection needs the iPhone locked and connected to power, and on a large library it can take up to a few days. For the steps in each iOS version, see how to delete duplicate photos on iPhone.

Delete the photos and videos you don't need

This is the step that frees the most space, and the one most people put off. Duplicates are only exact copies. The bigger pile is near-misses: five tries at the same group shot, blurry photos, screenshots of things you've already dealt with, and long videos you never watch.

You can do it in the Photos app with Select mode, which our guide to deleting photos on iPhone walks through. If the size of the job is what stops you, Keeper breaks it into one decision per photo: swipe right to keep, left to delete, with undo for a wrong swipe. You can limit a session to one album or one month, see the storage you freed when you tap Save, and pick up where you left off next time.

Deleted items stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days, so you can undo a mistake later. You can also delete them from there sooner.

Move photos off your iPhone

For photos you want to keep but don't need on your phone, Apple suggests you transfer them to a computer. Do this before you delete anything. If you use iCloud Photos, deleting a photo from the phone deletes it from iCloud too. So make sure the copy on your computer is a real file, not just a synced view. Apple also says your photo library isn't duplicated in your iCloud backup, so it's worth keeping a separate backup of your library.

Common questions

Does Optimize iPhone Storage delete my photos?

No. Your iPhone keeps space-saving versions of your photos and videos, and the full-resolution originals stay in iCloud.

Why don't I have a Duplicates album?

Either the Photos app hasn't found any duplicates or it hasn't finished looking. Detection needs the iPhone locked and connected to power, and it can take up to a few days on a large library.

How much free iCloud storage do I get?

Apple gives you 5 GB of iCloud storage for free when you set up iCloud. You can upgrade to iCloud+ for more.

Where can I see how much space my photos use?

Go to Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage. Photos is listed with the space it uses.
